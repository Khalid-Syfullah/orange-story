# Motion rules

The mechanical feel comes from four things: straight edges, things switching on, symmetric easing, and pixels that stay sharp while they move. These rules remove all four. Every effect, transition and rest state on the site follows them; every phase prompt points here.

1. **No straight fronts.** Every wipe or reveal is a distance field bitten by two decorrelated fbm fields at different scales: `front = d - t + n1*0.30 + n2*0.10`. A straight line reads as a wipe; a bitten one reads as something happening to the picture.
2. **Edges are printed, not faded.** Add a 4x4 Bayer threshold and a hash stipple at every boundary before the smoothstep, so breakup is a print screen, not an alpha ramp or flat bands.
3. **Nothing switches on.** Every treatment (texture, vignette, char, grain) is multiplied by the same driver that brings its colour in. There is never a frame where a texture appears over what was a clean frame.
4. **Ease into every pass.** The first 10–15% of any travelling effect ramps with smoothstep, so there is no edge to the effect starting. Use accelerating curves for things that fall or push in (`t*(lin + (1-lin)*t*t)`, lin 0.4–0.9) and keep ease-in-out for the few things that settle. Ease-in-out on everything is the metronome feel.
5. **The revealed picture arrives moving.** The new layer is offset in the direction the front travels by `lag = exp(-distBehindCut / d)` and settles as the front leaves it. A static image under a hole is a wipe; a picture still catching up with its edge is a reveal.
6. **Smear along motion.** Anything displaced is sampled with 5 taps along its displacement direction, signed the way it moves. Perfectly sharp pixels moving fast are the plainest tell of a wipe.
7. **Dolly, not scale.** A push-in is about the cut's own point, not the frame centre, and that point drifts a few percent sideways as the push accelerates. Soften radially from that point in the last stretch, the way things go soft near a lens.
8. **Bands travel with the front.** Char, ember, pith, glow exist only inside a band that moves with the cut: saturate a little way back, release further back. A term that saturates everywhere behind the front chars the whole frame on the first scroll tick.
9. **Breath is not a sine.** Rest states move by a sum of 2–3 incommensurate sines (periods like 5.3 s, 7.9 s, 11.4 s) plus an exp decay from the last event, at amplitudes of a few percent. One sine at a sixth of the value reads as the screen dimming.
10. **Hash, never period.** Slips, flickers, dot thresholds and speckles use `hash(band, floor(time*k))`, so no two repeats align. `fract(time/period)` on its own is a metronome.
11. **Scrub-safe by construction.** Every state is a function of smoothed progress `p`; scrolling backwards plays the transition in reverse with no pops. Frame sequences take the nearest decoded frame and decode ahead both ways.
12. **Seams get a mass.** A straight join between two halves reads as a mirror. Cover it with a soft anisotropic mass bitten by fbm, placed under the reveal rather than over it; ramp the displacement sign through zero across the seam so there is no discontinuity.
13. **Instances differ.** Clouds, dots and flecks have their own size, seed, drift speed (slower when larger) and phase; nothing is centred or evenly spaced.
14. **Even frame time.** Gate dead work: a front that has passed every pixel is identity, so skip it. A dropped frame mid-transition reads as mechanical too.
15. **Hold the still.** At any `p` the frame must be a picture someone would hang. If a mid-transition frame only makes sense as motion, the transition is a wipe.

## Scroll smoothing

- Intercept wheel (`preventDefault` unless `ctrlKey`), accumulate into `target`. Each frame: `current += (target - current) * (1 - exp(-dt / 180ms))`; snap when `|target - current| < 0.08px`. `velocity = current - previous`, exposed to the shader as `uVel` so smear and tilt follow the scroll's speed.
- On `pointer: coarse` or `prefers-reduced-motion`, leave native scroll on and smooth with a 60 ms constant.
- Keyboard and anchor jumps set `target`, never `current`.

## Rest states

- Pointer parallax 0.45% x / 0.32% y, approached with `current += (target - current) * 0.06` per frame; ambient drift 0.22% from two sines (0.17 Hz, 0.13 Hz).
- A scroll stop adds an exp-decay kick of about 1.5% to whatever breathes, dying in 1.2 s.
- The amplitudes live in `REST` (`src/stage/passes/rest.ts`); `coverRoom` reads them, so anything that moves a picture further knows how much slack the rest state already uses.

## The motion toolkit

Living paintings, built once (phase 17) for every chapter's own motion (`docs/MOTION-BRIEF.md`). The GLSL is at the end of `src/stage/gl/lib.glsl.ts`, prepended to every pass; the TypeScript twins, for DOM layers and for the uniforms the passes compute, are in `src/stage/motion.ts`; `e2e/motion.spec.ts` holds the contracts below. Each helper is:

- **Scrub-safe by construction.** A pure function of a painting point, of values the pass computes from `p`, and of the pass clock `tm = uTime * (1 - uStill)`. Nothing keeps state or reads scroll history, so a frame at rest is the same whichever way the scroll came (rule 11). Ambient motion (the wind) reads time only; the velocity response reads `uVel`, which is 0 at rest.
- **Still under reduced motion.** Each returns its rest value when `uStill` is 1: no sway, no layer offset, no velocity response, so the reduced-motion frame is the picture as it was before the toolkit.
- **Tuned outside GLSL.** Numbers come in as arguments from `src/content/art.ts` (per painting: `MASKS`, `sway`, `depth`) or the pass's `timeline.ts`, never typed into the shader.
- **Inside the frame.** Anything that moves a picture further than its framing does is bounded with `softBound` by the room `coverRoom` leaves (MOTION-BRIEF rule 3), and anything a soft mask drives stays under `FOLD_SHARE` of the mask's lattice cell, so the warp compresses but never folds or tears.

| Helper | GLSL | TS twin | What it gives | Bounds and rest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (a) Wind | `windAt(pt, tm, w)` | `windAt`, `windVec` | A sway at a painting point (height units): broad gusts carried downwind as two fields at different speeds and headings (so the pattern evolves as it travels), a finer, faster flutter riding on them, strongest in a gust. `w` = gust cells per painting height, cells a second, flutter scale, flutter speed (art.ts `Wind`). Fixed octaves: a phone sways as a desktop does. Rules 9 and 10: never a sine, never periodic | x spread about 0.55 (95% within -0.75..1.1, rarely past 1.6); y a third of it, with a lift as a leaf swings. 0 under `uStill` |
| (b) Masks | `foliageMask(c)`, `paleMask(c)`, `skyMask(c)`, `hueSat`, `bandMask`, `SOFT_MASK(tex, pt, cell, MASK)` | same names | What may move. Foliage: lit yellow-green to dark olive leaves (peel, gilt, bark, skin, sky out). Pale: a cream ground, linen and drapery in light, marble, paper. Sky: the painted blue. `SOFT_MASK` reads any mask on a lattice anchored to the painting and blends the four points round a pixel with a smoothstep weight: a continuous field (four taps) that moves with the painting | Bands in art.ts `MASKS`, measured on the real paintings (`docs/MOTION-AUDIT.md`, Masks). A full 0..1 change across one cell has slope 1.5 per cell |
| (c) Depth | `depthShift(near, nearOff, farOff)`, `softBound` | `depthShift`, `coverRoom`, `depthTravelMax`, `softBound` | Two layers from one painting: each point takes the far layer's offset where the near mask is 0 and the near layer's where it is 1, eased between, so near things slide over far ones as a warp, never a cut. Offsets are (rate - 1) times the camera's travel (art.ts `Depth`: `near`, `far`, `cell`) | The travel goes through `softBound(travel, depthTravelMax(...))`: neither layer leaves the room the framing leaves, the layers never part by more than `FOLD_SHARE` (0.4) of a cell. 0 under `uStill` |
| (d) Velocity | `velAmount(knee)`, `velSigned(knee)`, `velSmear(tex, pt, dir, len, knee)` | `velAmount`, `velSigned` | The scroll's speed as `1 - exp(-abs(uVel)/knee)`: linear for a slow wheel, saturating for a flick; signed with the scroll. `velSmear` samples a painting with the five-tap smear (rule 6) along the way the content moves, up to `len` for a flick | Always 0..1. 0 at rest and under `uStill`; it eases back with the scroll clock's own 180 ms smoothing, so nothing snaps when the scroll stops |
| (e) Growth | `growSettle(x, back, ramp)` | `growSettle`, `backFor`, `overshootOf` | An ease for things that grow: starts from rest (a smoothstep ramp over the first `ramp` of x, rule 4), passes 1, peaks at `1 + overshoot` and settles with no speed left. `backFor(0.06)` gives the `back` for a 6% overshoot | Exactly 0 at x <= 0 and exactly 1 at x >= 1, so a growth that has landed is the next segment's first frame. A function of x alone: scrubbing back shrinks it the same way |

Phase 18 adds three small helpers to the depth row (TS only, they compute uniforms): `orbitTravelMax` (the bound for a camera travel the painting's pan does *not* carry, like the hero's orbit: the layers move `near` and `far` times the travel from rest, so the lead is the larger rate itself), `swayRoom` (the share of a frame's room a leaf sway may use when it shares the room with a depth layer, inside the fold), and, in the passes, the *eroded* layer mask: a lattice node reads the least of the mask at the node and at two taps half a cell away on a diagonal (`hero/frag.ts` `skyNode`, `model/frag.ts` `groveCloudNode`), so a feature smaller than a cell (a face, a leaf, a stem) is never left in the wrong layer by a node that fell in the sky beside it; the soft hand-over between the layers lies in the flat sky or cloud, where a warp does not show. A layer that moves no faster than the camera (rates in 0..1: the grove's cloudscape) needs no room of its own: its net travel is `rate x camera`, inside what the camera's move already uses.

Proven on one painting each (phase 17, `docs/MOTION-AUDIT.md`): the graft in two depth layers (the cream ground far at 0.5x, the hands, branch and leaves near at 1.8x of the camera's travel), and the canopy's leaves in the wind (foliage mask times `windAt`, about 4 px of sway, 8 px in a gust at 1440x900). Under reduced motion both frames are the frames before the toolkit.
