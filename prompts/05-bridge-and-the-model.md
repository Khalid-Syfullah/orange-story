# Phase 5 of 16: The bridge and Chapter 1, The Model

You are building orange.io. The hero (phase 4) is painted. This phase carries the visitor from the hero film into the story: the bridge that transforms the scene in place, the static handover, and the four beats of The Model with their copy.

## What the visitor sees, in order

The hero is a monumental orange on a marble plinth under deep cobalt, with its headline, subline and button on the left, a small rainbow glint near the type, and faint dotted rings rippling outward from the orange like a signal. As the scroll begins the camera tilts up and the scene transforms in place rather than cutting. The plinth turns to gnarled bark from the bottom up; its top slab stays put, then lifts away like a cap. Leaves sprout along new branches, a grafting knife falls past the lens, and the rings keep pulsing across the sky. The camera drops along a branch to a pair of hands in a gold-embroidered blue velvet cuff winding raffia round a fresh graft; behind them the cobalt has given way to flat paper-white and the knife lies on the bough. The camera pushes through the scion's leaves into a dense orange canopy ("We build it."), drops to a hand picking a single gilded orange ("We rank it."), and cuts on that orange to a young man in green velvet with auburn curls, who slices it with a knife while a second hand comes in from the left and takes one half ("We share in what it earns."). The halves are held a hand's width apart: that is the frame The Split opens. Throughout, a thin hairline grid with small crosshair markers, the Orange mark top left, "The Model" on the left rail and the Apply pill top right stay fixed over the painting, and each beat's copy fades in as its painting settles.

## Task

2. **Bridge reel (in-place transformation):** in the `bridge` segment scrub the `bridge` sequence (121 frames) by local progress through the texture manager's `aim`/`near`, framed by the same `cover()` math as the hero. Add a per-frame pan and tilt table to `art.ts` (default zero; vertical tilt keyed to the reel's frames so the camera's upward tilt is carried by the table, not by guesswork), so the orange never jumps between film and reel and leaves the bottom of the frame only as the tilt carries it.
3. **Rings keep pulsing:** the hero's dotted rings (phase 4) continue into the bridge from the same origin (the orange's crown, `uncover(art.hero.crown)` each frame so it holds at any viewport) while the reel plays: same code, same dots, their own phase per ring (rule 13), drifting outward with the scroll. Their colour moves from milk toward ink in step with the reel's whiteness (measure it from the frame's mean luminance, smoothed) and they fade out by the time the knife is at rest. Nothing pops on or off (rule 3).
4. **Static handover:** the shader hands the hero loop to the reel through the static treatment exactly as `docs/TRANSITIONS.md` describes: a diagonal wave from the bottom-right corner, aspect-corrected to run at 45°, bent by slow noise; film ahead, reel behind; at the crest a horizontal tear with hashed row shifts, a mosaic that starts coarse (24px cells) and resolves to 1px as the crest passes, a chroma split of ±0.006 along x, and a 5×5 bitmap-font ASCII rubbing of the luminance inside the crest band only (glyphs from arithmetic: five 5-bit rows per glyph, ordered lightest to heaviest ` .:-+=*%#@`). Tear amplitudes scale with `|uVel|` so a fast scroll tears harder. Fast paths: pixels provably ahead of or behind the crest skip the treatment.
5. **The four beats:** `graft`, `grove` (two beats of its own), `share` in the `model` segment, in this order and with these shares of its range: graft 0.00–0.22 (no copy), canopy 0.22–0.46, picking 0.46–0.66, share 0.66–1.00.
